Who we are

Care built around dignity, trust, and independence.

Finding care for someone you love can feel like a big decision. Our role is to make that decision a little easier by providing dependable, compassionate support built around your loved one's routines, comfort, and independence. Whether your family needs a few hours of help or more consistent assistance, we want you to feel confident knowing someone reliable is there when you cannot be.

We help with everyday needs while respecting each person's preferences, privacy, and dignity. More than anything, we want to be a familiar, dependable resource families can turn to when they need an extra helping hand at home.

How we can help

Support for everyday life at home

When everyday routines become harder to manage alone, a little dependable help can make it easier for your loved one to remain comfortable, connected, and independent at home. San Antonio Caregivers provides non-medical support ranging from companionship and household assistance to hands-on personal assistance services.

Companionship

Helpful conversation, activities, and everyday interaction that support connection and engagement.

  • Assistance with hobbies and crafts
  • Reading and writing
  • Taking walks
  • Social activities
  • Friendly conversation

Meal Preparation

Help keeping regular meals and grocery routines manageable.

  • Meal preparation
  • Grocery shopping
  • Meal planning

Light Housekeeping

Everyday household help focused on keeping the client's living space comfortable and manageable.

  • Dusting surfaces
  • Vacuuming and mopping
  • Cleaning bathrooms
  • Laundry, excluding ironing
  • Watering house plants
  • General tidying-up

Transportation & Errands

Help getting to important appointments, activities, and everyday destinations.

  • Running errands
  • Transportation to appointments
  • Transportation to church
  • Transportation to social activities

Pet Care

Simple pet-related help that supports the client's everyday routine.

  • Walking pets
  • Feeding pets
  • Providing fresh water

Home Monitoring

A dependable presence when the client needs help coordinating activity around the home.

  • Supervising home maintenance
  • Waiting for service technicians
  • Being present when outside service providers visit

Personal Assistance Services

Personal Assistance Services include companionship and household support as well as assistance with everyday personal care needs.

  • Feeding
  • Transferring
  • Toileting
  • Incontinence care
  • Assistance with ambulation and exercise
  • Grooming
  • Bathing
  • Dressing
  • Routine care of hair and skin
  • Assistance with self-administered medications
  • Hands-on care or assistance
  • Taking temperature
  • Taking blood pressure using an electronic cuff

Respite Care

Temporary, dependable support for family caregivers who need time to rest, work, run errands, attend appointments, or simply recharge.

Respite care may be helpful when:

  • A family caregiver needs a few hours away
  • Work or family responsibilities make it difficult to be present
  • A loved one should not be left alone for an extended period
  • A family needs temporary support during a particularly demanding period

Family caregiver support

Support that works alongside your family

You can still be the primary support — you just don't have to do everything yourself.

Many families are not looking to hand over care completely. They may simply need someone dependable to step in for a few hours, help with certain routines, provide companionship, or be there when work, appointments, children, distance, or everyday responsibilities make it difficult to be present.

San Antonio Caregivers can work alongside your family to provide the extra support that helps keep your loved one comfortable, connected, and independent at home — while giving you more flexibility and peace of mind.

Ways we can help fill the gaps

  • A few hours of respite so you can rest, run errands, work, or take care of other responsibilities.
  • Companionship and supervision when your loved one should not be alone for an extended period.
  • Help with morning or evening routines, including dressing, grooming, meals, and mobility assistance.
  • Transportation and errands when you cannot make every appointment or outing.
  • Meal preparation and light household support to make everyday routines more manageable.
  • Personal assistance when your loved one needs hands-on help with activities of daily living.
  • Flexible ongoing support that can complement what family members are already providing.

Care built around the role your family already plays

Our goal is not to replace the family's involvement. We learn what you already handle, where the difficult gaps are, and what routines matter most to your loved one. From there, care can be built around the areas where an extra set of dependable hands would make the biggest difference.

Getting started

You do not need to know exactly what kind of care you need before calling.

Start with a conversation

Tell us what is happening, what is becoming difficult, and when your family could use support. We will listen, answer questions, and help you think through your options without pressure or commitment.

Build a plan around your loved one

We learn about the client's routines, preferences, abilities, schedule, and the areas where support would be most helpful, then work with the family to identify an appropriate type and schedule of assistance.

Begin care and adjust as needed

Care begins according to the agreed schedule, with support centered on safety, dignity, consistency, and independence. As needs change, families can stay in communication so the plan can be reviewed and adjusted.

Our guiding principles

Our care approach

Care that fits the person — not the other way around.

Every family's situation is different. Our approach begins with understanding the person receiving care, the routines that matter to them, and the areas where assistance would make everyday life safer, easier, or more comfortable.

Personalized Support

We take time to understand each client's routines, preferences, abilities, schedule, and comfort level. Care is planned around the individual rather than expecting the individual to adapt to a one-size-fits-all routine.

Supporting Independence

Our caregivers are there to assist, not unnecessarily take over. Whenever appropriate, clients are encouraged to remain involved in their routines and activities so they can maintain confidence, familiarity, and as much independence as possible.

Dignity in Personal Care

When hands-on assistance is needed, caregivers provide help with privacy, patience, and respect. Personal care may include assistance with bathing, grooming, dressing, toileting, mobility, transferring, and other activities of daily living included in the client's service plan.

Companionship & Everyday Support

Care may also include conversation, hobbies, meal preparation, light housekeeping, transportation, errands, medication reminders as permitted, and other non-medical support identified in the client's service plan.

Respite for Family Caregivers

Respite care gives family members a dependable way to step away temporarily while knowing their loved one has support. It can be used occasionally or as part of an ongoing schedule depending on the family's needs and service availability.

Support During Changing Care Needs

Some families need additional non-medical support when a loved one is living with dementia, experiencing changes in memory or behavior, or receiving hospice services from another provider. When appropriate to the client's service plan, San Antonio Caregivers can provide companionship and personal assistance while working within the family's established care routine.

Ongoing Communication

Needs may change over time. Families are encouraged to stay in communication with our team so questions, scheduling concerns, and changes in care needs can be addressed appropriately.

Professional care standards

Compassion matters. So do professionalism, accountability, and trust.

Inviting someone into your home to help care for a loved one requires trust. Our caregivers are expected to follow professional standards designed to protect the client, the family, and the caregiving relationship.

Professional Boundaries

Caregivers are expected to maintain respectful, professional relationships with clients and families.

Privacy & Confidentiality

Client information, personal matters, and care-related information are treated as private and confidential.

Documented Care

Caregivers document services provided and relevant observations during each shift according to company procedures.

Training & Safety

Caregivers receive training related to client safety, infection control, emergency procedures, confidentiality, professional boundaries, and other responsibilities associated with their assignments.

Agency Accountability

Caregivers remain accountable to San Antonio Caregivers and are expected to report client concerns, safety issues, incidents, and meaningful changes that may affect care.

Caregiver-Client Matching

Assignments are made with consideration for the client's needs as well as the caregiver's skills, abilities, and suitability for the assignment.
